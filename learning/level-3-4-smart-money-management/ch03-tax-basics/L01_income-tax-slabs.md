# Income Tax Slabs
**Level:** 3-4 Smart Money Management | **Chapter:** 3 - Tax Basics | **Topic:** 1

## Key Concepts
- New Tax Regime (2024–25)
- Tax Slabs
- Standard Deduction
- Filing Deadline

## Quick Spark
India's new tax regime (2024–25) charges no tax up to ₹3 lakh, then rising rates in slabs up to 30% above ₹15 lakh — plus a rebate that can make income up to about ₹7 lakh effectively tax-free. A ₹75,000 standard deduction reduces taxable salary automatically, and returns must be filed by July 31st each year.

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: New Tax Regime (2024–25) — A Simpler Way to Pay Tax
The new tax regime is a simplified income tax structure offering lower rates but fewer deductions and exemptions compared to the older "old regime." It works by taxing income in slabs — different portions of income taxed at different rates — and applies automatically unless a taxpayer actively chooses the old regime instead. This exists because many taxpayers found the old system, with its many deduction categories to track, complicated, so the new regime trades some deductions for simpler, generally lower slab rates. So knowing "which regime am I in" matters because it changes both how much tax is owed and how the return should be filed.
Example: CA Jay explains to clients: "If you don't actively choose the old regime, the new regime applies by default — check your payslip and Form 16 to know which one you're under."

LEARNING POINT 2: Tax Slabs — Paying Different Rates on Different Portions of Income
A tax slab system divides total income into ranges and taxes each range at its own rate — only the portion of income falling into a higher slab is taxed at the higher rate, not the entire income. Under the new regime (2024–25): up to ₹3 lakh is nil, ₹3–7 lakh is 5%, ₹7–10 lakh is 10%, ₹10–12 lakh is 15%, ₹12–15 lakh is 20%, and above ₹15 lakh is 30%. This keeps the system fair to lower earners — someone earning ₹8 lakh isn't taxed at 10% on their entire income, only on the ₹1 lakh that falls in the ₹7–10 lakh slab, with the rest taxed at the lower rates that apply to those earlier portions. So the common myth that "earning more pushes your whole income into a higher bracket" is false — only the incremental portion above each threshold is taxed at the higher rate.
Example: If Maanu (once she starts earning) makes ₹8 lakh a year, she pays nil on the first ₹3 lakh, 5% on the next ₹4 lakh, and 10% only on the remaining ₹1 lakh — not 10% on the whole ₹8 lakh.

LEARNING POINT 3: Standard Deduction — An Automatic Reduction for Salaried Taxpayers
The standard deduction is a flat amount — ₹75,000 under the new regime for salaried individuals — automatically subtracted from gross salary to arrive at taxable income, before slab rates are applied. It works simply because it requires no receipts or investment proof; it's a blanket reduction available to every eligible salaried taxpayer, unlike many other deductions that need supporting documents. This exists to give salaried employees some basic tax relief even under the new regime, which otherwise removes many other deduction options available in the old regime. So it directly lowers the income on which tax is calculated, meaning the effective tax-free threshold for many salaried people ends up higher than the ₹3 lakh nil slab alone suggests.
Example: If Dia's gross salary is ₹5,00,000, her taxable salary effectively drops to ₹4,25,000 after the ₹75,000 standard deduction — before any slab rates are calculated.

LEARNING POINT 4: Filing Deadline — Why "July 31st" Matters
The filing deadline is the last date for submitting your income tax return (ITR) for a financial year, and for most individual taxpayers in India (who don't require an audit), that date is July 31st of the following year. It works as a legal cutoff — filing after this date can attract late fees, interest on unpaid tax, and can limit the ability to carry forward certain losses to future years. This deadline exists to keep the tax system running on a predictable annual cycle, giving the government the information it needs and taxpayers a clear window to file correctly. So treating July 31st as a hard deadline, not a suggestion, avoids unnecessary penalties and stress, even though the government occasionally extends it in exceptional circumstances.
Example: CA Jay reminds every client every June: "Don't wait until July 30th to start gathering your documents — start in May, and July 31st becomes easy instead of a scramble."

## Real-Life Fact
Under Section 87A of the Income Tax Act, taxpayers under the new regime can claim a tax rebate that can make total income up to approximately ₹7 lakh effectively tax-free, even though the slab structure itself starts charging tax from ₹3 lakh onward — the rebate cancels out that tax for eligible taxpayers within this limit.
The new tax regime has been the default regime for individual taxpayers in India since Financial Year 2023–24, meaning taxpayers must actively opt for the old regime if they prefer it.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing that moving into a higher tax slab means your entire income gets taxed at that higher rate.
Correction: Only the portion of income that falls within a particular slab is taxed at that slab's rate — the rest is still taxed at the lower rates that apply to the earlier portions of your income. Earning more never means "losing money overall" purely because of slabs.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Tax slabs are stepped, not flat — you're never taxed at one rate on your whole income, only progressively more on each higher portion you earn.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. Under the new tax regime (2024–25), income up to what amount is taxed at nil?
A. ₹1 lakh
B. ₹3 lakh
C. ₹7 lakh
D. ₹15 lakh
Hint: Recall the first slab in the new regime.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: The first slab, ₹0 to ₹3 lakh, is taxed at nil under the new regime for 2024–25.

Q2. What is the standard deduction available to salaried taxpayers under the new regime (2024–25)?
A. ₹50,000
B. ₹75,000
C. ₹1,00,000
D. ₹1,50,000
Hint: This is a flat, no-proof-required deduction from salary.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: The standard deduction under the new regime for 2024–25 is ₹75,000, automatically reduced from gross salary before tax calculation.

Q3. Vikram earns ₹10,00,000 in taxable income under the new regime. Which slabs will apply to portions of his income?
A. Only the 30% slab, since his income exceeds ₹7 lakh
B. Nil, 5%, and 10% slabs applied progressively to different portions of his income
C. A single flat rate of 10% on the entire ₹10,00,000
D. No tax at all, since he's under ₹15 lakh
Hint: Remember slabs are stepped, not flat.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Vikram's income passes through the nil slab (up to ₹3 lakh), the 5% slab (₹3–7 lakh), and the 10% slab (₹7–10 lakh), each portion taxed at its own rate.

Q4. Why might someone with a taxable income of exactly ₹7,00,000 end up paying no tax at all under the new regime, despite slabs technically applying from ₹3 lakh onward?
A. Because ₹7 lakh is below the nil slab
B. Because of the Section 87A rebate, which can offset tax for income up to around ₹7 lakh
C. Because the government waives tax for round numbers
D. Because standard deduction alone brings tax to zero at any income level
Hint: Recall the rebate mentioned in the Real-Life Fact, separate from the slabs themselves.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: The Section 87A rebate is separate from the slab structure — it can cancel out tax liability entirely for eligible taxpayers with income up to roughly ₹7 lakh.

Q5. CA Jay has a client with taxable salary income (after standard deduction) of ₹11,50,000, who wants to know the consequence of missing the July 31st filing deadline. What should CA Jay warn about?
A. Nothing, since deadlines are only suggestions
B. Possible late fees, interest on any unpaid tax, and restrictions on carrying forward certain losses
C. Automatic tax exemption for late filers
D. A mandatory shift to the old tax regime as punishment
Hint: Recall why the filing deadline is treated as a hard cutoff, not a suggestion.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Filing after July 31st can result in late fees, interest on unpaid tax, and restrictions on carrying forward certain losses to offset future income.

## Explore Further
Q1. What section of India's Income Tax Act governs the rebate that can make income up to roughly ₹7 lakh tax-free under the new regime?
A. Section 80C
B. Section 87A
C. Section 10(14)
D. Section 44AD
Hint: Recall the section referenced in the Real-Life Fact.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Section 87A provides a tax rebate to eligible resident individual taxpayers, which under the new regime can bring effective tax liability to zero for income up to approximately ₹7 lakh.

Q2. Since which financial year has the new tax regime been the default option for individual taxpayers in India?
A. FY 2018-19
B. FY 2020-21
C. FY 2023-24
D. FY 2025-26
Hint: Recall the Real-Life Fact about when the new regime became default.
Correct Answer: C
Fact / Concept Explanation: The new tax regime became the default regime starting Financial Year 2023–24, meaning anyone wanting the old regime's deductions must actively select it while filing.

Q3. Why does the government offer two tax regimes (old and new) instead of just one?
A. To confuse taxpayers on purpose
B. To let taxpayers choose between lower rates with fewer deductions (new) or higher rates with more deduction options (old), based on what benefits them more
C. Because the old regime was declared illegal
D. Because only businesses can use the new regime
Hint: Think about the trade-off between rates and deductions covered earlier.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The dual-regime system accommodates different taxpayer situations — someone with many eligible deductions may benefit more from the old regime, while someone with few deductions often benefits from the new regime's lower rates.

Q4. What government body is primarily responsible for administering and collecting income tax in India?
A. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
B. Income Tax Department (under the Central Board of Direct Taxes)
C. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
D. National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)
Hint: Think about which authority processes income tax returns, not banking or markets regulation.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Income Tax Department, functioning under the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), administers direct tax laws in India, including processing returns and enforcing the slab system.

Q5. If a salaried taxpayer under the new regime has additional income sources (like savings account interest) beyond salary, how does this generally affect their tax calculation?
A. It would be ignored entirely, since only salary is taxed
B. It would typically be added to total income and taxed according to the same slab structure, on top of salary income
C. It would automatically be taxed at 30% regardless of amount
D. It would eliminate the standard deduction entirely
Hint: Think about how "total income" is built up from multiple sources before slabs are applied.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Income tax in India is generally calculated on total income from all applicable sources, combined and then taxed according to the applicable slab rates — not calculated separately per source.
