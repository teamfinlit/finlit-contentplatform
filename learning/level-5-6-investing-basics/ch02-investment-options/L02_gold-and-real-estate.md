# Gold & Real Estate
**Level:** 5-6 Investing Basics | **Chapter:** 2 - Investment Options | **Topic:** 2

## Key Concepts
- Gold as Investment
- Real Estate = Home
- SGBs (Sovereign Gold Bonds)
- Digital Gold
- Rental Income
- Appreciation
- REITs

## Quick Spark
Gold acts as a financial shield against inflation and a weakening currency over decades. Real estate builds wealth through rental income and property values rising over time. Modern options like Gold ETFs and Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) let you own gold without worrying about storing it. And Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) let anyone invest in big commercial buildings with a small amount of money!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: Gold as an Investment & Modern Gold Formats (SGBs, Gold ETFs & Digital Gold)
For generations, gold has been a "safe-haven" asset that protects wealth during high inflation, economic trouble and a falling currency, and over long periods it has grown by around 10% to 11% a year in rupee terms. But jewellery and gold coins come with storage worries, the risk of theft, and making charges. Modern formats solve these problems. Gold ETFs, traded on the stock exchange and regulated by SEBI, let you invest in pure gold with small amounts. Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs), issued by the RBI on behalf of the government, pay 2.5% interest a year on top of any rise in the gold price; the government has not issued new SGBs since February 2024, but existing bonds still trade on the stock exchange. Digital gold sold through apps can start from ₹100, but SEBI warned in November 2025 that it is unregulated, so investors get none of the protections that apply to regulated products.
Example: Instead of buying gold coins and renting an expensive bank locker, Dia invested in Sovereign Gold Bonds when the government issued them. She earns 2.5% interest every year while the value of her bonds follows the gold price.

LEARNING POINT 2: Real Estate — Rental Income vs. Appreciation
Investing in real estate (such as flats or land) builds wealth in two ways: regular rent from tenants, and appreciation as the property's value rises over the years. But buying property needs a large amount of money upfront, involves legal paperwork, and locks your money into an asset that is hard to sell quickly (illiquid). Home loan EMIs can also strain a family's budget if the property sits empty. And rental income in India is fairly modest — homes typically earn only 2% to 4% of their value in rent each year.
Example: Maanu's family owns a flat in the city that they rent out. They collect rent from a tenant every month, while the flat's market value steadily rises over 15 years.

LEARNING POINT 3: REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)
A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) works like a mutual fund for commercial property. It pools money from thousands of small investors to own office parks, tech parks and shopping malls, collects rent from large company tenants, and must pay out at least 90% of its net distributable cash flow to unitholders. REIT units trade on the stock exchange, so anyone can become a commercial property investor for the price of a single unit — usually a few hundred rupees — without managing buildings or dealing with property brokers.
Example: CA Jay helps Maanu invest ₹1,000 in an Indian REIT listed on the stock exchange. She now owns a tiny share of large IT office parks in Mumbai and Bengaluru and receives payouts every quarter.

## Real-Life Fact
SEBI rules require Indian Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) to distribute at least 90% of their net distributable cash flow to unitholders, at least twice a year — which is why REITs give investors regular payouts.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing that gold jewellery is the best way to invest in gold because you can also wear it.
Correction: Jewellery comes with making charges and wastage costs of around 10% to 25% that you don't get back when you sell it. Regulated options like Gold ETFs (and existing Sovereign Gold Bonds) give you pure gold value without making charges or locker fees.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Gold protects your buying power during a crisis, while modern options like Gold ETFs, SGBs and REITs make owning these assets easy and income-generating.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. Why do investors keep some gold in their long-term investments?
A. Gold is guaranteed to triple in price every 3 months
B. Gold has historically protected against high inflation, market uncertainty and a weakening currency
C. Gold replaces the need for food and water
D. Only commercial banks can hold gold
Hint: Gold has traditionally kept its buying power during a crisis.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Gold is seen as a safe-haven asset that holds its value during inflation and market turmoil.

Q2. What extra yearly interest do Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) pay on top of following the gold price?
A. A 10% cash bonus
B. 2.5% simple interest a year, paid into the investor's bank account
C. Free jewellery delivered every year
D. Nothing at all
Hint: SGBs pay a fixed 2.5% a year on the original investment amount.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: SGBs pay a fixed 2.5% a year on the original issue price, credited every six months, in addition to any rise in the gold price. This interest is taxable.

Q3. What are the two main ways investors make money from property?
A. Bank lottery rewards and tax refunds
B. Regular rental income and the property's value rising over time
C. Gold dividends and factory output
D. Government pension allowances
Hint: Think of rent from tenants and rising land values.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Real estate earns money through rent and through appreciation as property values rise.

Q4. What is a big drawback of buying a home as an investment compared with financial assets?
A. A home can be sold in 2 seconds on a phone app
B. It needs a lot of money upfront, is hard to sell quickly, and usually earns only 2% to 4% a year in rent
C. Homes can never be rented out
D. Banks don't allow anyone to own a home
Hint: Buying a flat takes big savings, is slow to sell, and earns a low yearly rent.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Property needs a lot of capital, has high buying and selling costs, can't be sold instantly (it is illiquid), and earns low rental yields in India (2% to 4%).

Q5. What is a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT)?
A. A government office that builds roads
B. An investment trust that pools money to own rent-earning commercial property and pays the rental income out to its unitholders
C. A loan taken to buy furniture
D. A tax charged on shopping
Hint: It lets you own a small piece of office parks, like a mutual fund.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: REITs let ordinary investors own units in rent-earning commercial property and receive regular payouts.

## Explore Further
Q1. What share of its net distributable cash flow must an Indian REIT pay out to unitholders under SEBI rules?
A. At least 90%
B. Exactly 10%
C. 50%
D. Zero
Hint: SEBI requires most of the rental earnings to be paid out to investors.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: SEBI's REIT regulations require at least 90% of net distributable cash flow to be paid to unitholders, at least once every six months.

Q2. How are capital gains on a Sovereign Gold Bond taxed if an individual bought it when the RBI first issued it and holds it until it matures after 8 years?
A. The gains are completely tax-free
B. They are taxed at a flat 50% penalty rate
C. They attract a 30% wealth tax
D. The bond turns into government debt with no payout
Hint: The government gives a full tax exemption to original buyers who hold until maturity.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Capital gains when an SGB is redeemed at maturity are tax-free for individuals who bought it at original issue. Since Budget 2026 (for redemptions from 1 April 2026), people who bought SGBs on the stock exchange no longer get this exemption.

Q3. What is "rental yield" in real estate?
A. The total weight of the building in tonnes
B. The yearly rent a property earns, shown as a percentage of the property's price
C. The interest rate on a home loan
D. The cost of painting the building
Hint: Calculate (Yearly Rent ÷ Property Price) × 100.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Rental yield is the yearly rent divided by the property's value, which shows how much income the property earns for its price.

Q4. How does a Gold ETF (Exchange Traded Fund) follow the price of physical gold?
A. By investing in crop seeds
B. By holding physical gold of 99.5% purity in secure vaults, with each ETF unit representing a small amount of that gold
C. By guessing gold prices with computer games
D. By printing coupons with gold ink
Hint: Gold ETFs back their units with real gold stored in vaults.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Gold ETFs hold standard physical gold (99.5% purity) with a custodian, so investors can buy and sell gold units easily on the stock exchange.

Q5. What is the main advantage of investing in commercial property through REIT units instead of buying a whole building?
A. REIT units can be bought and sold within seconds on the stock exchange during market hours, at clear market prices
B. REIT units need registry papers signed at the municipal office every week
C. REIT units can only be sold once every 50 years
D. REITs never pay anything out
Hint: REIT units trade on the stock exchange just like company shares.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Because REIT units trade on the stock exchange like shares, they can be sold quickly at a transparent price, without the long process of selling a building.
