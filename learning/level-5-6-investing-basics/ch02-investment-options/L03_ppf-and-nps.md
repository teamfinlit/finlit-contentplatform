# PPF & NPS
**Level:** 5-6 Investing Basics | **Chapter:** 2 - Investment Options | **Topic:** 3

## Key Concepts
- Public Provident Fund (PPF)
- National Pension System (NPS)
- 15-Year Lock-in
- EEE Tax-Free Status
- Retirement Focus
- Extra ₹50k Deduction

## Quick Spark
PPF is a government-backed savings scheme with a 15-year lock-in, and its interest and maturity amount are completely tax-free. NPS is a retirement plan that invests across shares, company bonds and government bonds. Under the old tax regime, NPS also gives an extra ₹50,000 tax deduction on top of the usual ₹1.5 lakh. Using both together builds a strong base for long-term wealth and a secure retirement!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: Public Provident Fund (PPF) — Safe, Long-Term, Tax-Free Wealth
The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a trusted savings scheme backed by the Government of India, designed for long-term saving. It runs for 15 years (and can be extended in 5-year blocks), and you can deposit up to ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year. PPF has EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax status: under the old tax regime your deposits can be claimed as a tax deduction (Section 80C of the old Income-tax Act, now Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025); the interest earned every year is tax-free; and the full amount you withdraw at maturity is tax-free. You can make partial withdrawals from the 7th financial year for specific needs.
Example: Dia opens a PPF account for Rudy and deposits money every year. She knows every rupee of interest added over the 15 years is tax-free and backed by the Government of India.

LEARNING POINT 2: National Pension System (NPS) — Structured Retirement Planning
The National Pension System (NPS) is a regulated, market-linked pension scheme built to grow a large retirement fund. Your contributions are invested across a mix of asset classes: equity (shares, for higher growth), corporate bonds (steady interest) and government securities (safety). Because the money is mostly locked in until age 60, NPS builds lifelong saving discipline, stops you from spending it early, and lets your fund compound over your whole working life.
Example: CA Jay contributes to his NPS account regularly, choosing 50% equity and 50% debt, and lets compounding build his retirement fund while he works.

LEARNING POINT 3: NPS Tax Benefits & What Happens at Retirement
If you use the old tax regime, NPS gives an extra tax deduction of up to ₹50,000 (Section 80CCD(1B) of the old Income-tax Act), on top of the usual ₹1.5 lakh limit — which makes it a powerful tax-saver. These deductions are not available under the new tax regime, which is now the default. At age 60, most NPS members (those not in government jobs) can take up to 80% of their fund as a lump sum if the fund is above ₹12 lakh, and must use at least 20% to buy an annuity that pays a regular pension for life. Only up to 60% of the fund is tax-free when withdrawn; any lump sum above that is taxable. Government employees keep the earlier rule: up to 60% as a lump sum and at least 40% in an annuity.
Example: Maanu starts contributing to NPS as soon as she starts earning. Under the old tax regime she can reduce her taxable income by an extra ₹50,000 a year, while building towards a monthly pension in the future.

## Real-Life Fact
EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) is the most tax-friendly treatment in Indian tax law: no tax when you invest (under the old regime), no tax as interest builds up, and no tax when you withdraw at maturity. PPF is one of the few investments that gets it.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Avoiding PPF and NPS just because they lock your money in for many years.
Correction: Long lock-ins are a feature, not a flaw. They build saving discipline, stop panic selling, and protect your money so compounding can keep working until retirement.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Use PPF for guaranteed, tax-free stability and NPS for market-linked retirement growth with extra tax savings.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. How long does a Public Provident Fund (PPF) account run before it matures?
A. 1 year
B. 15 years (can be extended in 5-year blocks)
C. 50 years
D. 3 months
Hint: It is a long-term 15-year plan for building family wealth.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: A PPF account has a 15-year term, which can be extended in blocks of 5 years as many times as you like.

Q2. What does the "EEE" tax status of PPF stand for?
A. Expensive, Excessive, Extravagant
B. Exempt when you invest, Exempt on the interest earned, and Exempt when you withdraw at maturity
C. Electronic Entry Every day
D. Equity, Economy, Enterprise
Hint: You pay no tax at any stage of the investment.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: EEE stands for Exempt-Exempt-Exempt: the amount invested (deductible under the old regime), the interest earned, and the maturity amount are all free of tax.

Q3. What is the most you can deposit in a PPF account in one financial year?
A. ₹10,000
B. ₹1.5 lakh
C. ₹50 lakh
D. No limit
Hint: The yearly maximum is the same as the main tax-deduction limit.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: The maximum deposit allowed in a PPF account in a single financial year is ₹1.5 lakh.

Q4. Under the old tax regime, what extra tax deduction does the National Pension System (NPS) give beyond the usual ₹1.5 lakh limit?
A. An extra deduction of up to ₹50,000 (Section 80CCD(1B) of the old Income-tax Act)
B. Free airline tickets once a year
C. 100% discount on groceries
D. No tax on imported sports cars
Hint: NPS gives an extra ₹50,000 deduction on top of the usual limit.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Under the old tax regime, NPS contributions get an extra deduction of up to ₹50,000, separate from the ₹1.5 lakh limit. This deduction is not available under the new tax regime.

Q5. At age 60, what is the most of the NPS fund that can be withdrawn TAX-FREE as a lump sum?
A. Exactly 10%
B. Up to 60% (any lump sum above that is taxable)
C. 0%
D. 100%, but only if spent within 24 hours
Hint: Six-tenths of the fund can come out tax-free.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Up to 60% of the NPS fund can be withdrawn tax-free at 60. Since December 2025, non-government members with a fund above ₹12 lakh can take up to 80% as a lump sum (the extra 20% is taxable) and must put at least 20% into an annuity; government employees keep the 60% lump sum and 40% annuity rule.

## Explore Further
Q1. Which body regulates the National Pension System (NPS) in India?
A. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
B. Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA)
C. Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI)
D. Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)
Hint: Look for the regulator set up specifically for pensions.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority) is the statutory body that regulates and supervises NPS in India.

Q2. In NPS, what is the difference between "Active Choice" and "Auto Choice"?
A. Active Choice lets you choose your own mix of equity, corporate bonds and government securities, while Auto Choice changes the mix automatically as you get older
B. Auto Choice invests everything in lottery tickets
C. Active Choice is only for government employees
D. Auto Choice requires you to trade shares by hand every day
Hint: Auto Choice uses an age-based plan to manage risk for you.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Active Choice lets you set your own asset mix (usually up to 75% in equity), while Auto Choice automatically reduces equity and increases safer assets as you age.

Q3. When can you first make a partial withdrawal from a PPF account?
A. 24 hours after opening it
B. From the 7th financial year onwards (within set limits)
C. Never
D. Only on weekends
Hint: Partial withdrawals open up after 6 full financial years.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Partial withdrawals from PPF are allowed from the 7th financial year after the account was opened, up to set limits.

Q4. What is an "annuity" in NPS retirement planning?
A. A one-time penalty paid to the government
B. A contract with an insurance company that turns a lump sum into a guaranteed regular income for life
C. A short-term bank loan
D. A yearly tax audit for senior citizens
Hint: It turns your savings into a monthly pension.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: An annuity pays you a regular, guaranteed income through retirement in exchange for a lump sum paid upfront.

Q5. Why is PPF considered to have almost zero risk of not being paid back?
A. Because PPF money is invested in foreign tech shares
B. Because it is a government scheme backed directly by the Central Government of India
C. Because banks guarantee it with silver
D. Because foreign central banks insure it
Hint: The Central Government itself guarantees both the money and the interest.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: PPF is backed by the Central Government of India, making it one of the safest savings options in the country.
