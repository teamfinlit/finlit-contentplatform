# Fixed Deposits & Bonds
**Level:** 5-6 Investing Basics | **Chapter:** 2 - Investment Options | **Topic:** 1

## Quick Spark
Fixed Deposits (FDs) lock your money in a bank for a set term at a guaranteed interest rate. Bonds are loans you give to governments or companies in exchange for regular interest payments. Government bonds offer high safety, while corporate bonds pay more interest in return for a little more risk. And bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions, like a seesaw!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: Safe & Steady — Fixed Deposits (FDs)
A Fixed Deposit is a simple bank product: you leave a sum of money with a bank for a fixed period (the tenure) and the bank pays you a fixed, guaranteed interest rate. Major Indian banks currently pay roughly 6% to 7% a year, and senior citizens (aged 60 and above) usually get about 0.5% extra. FDs keep your money safe and your returns predictable, but the interest you earn is fully taxable at your income tax slab rate. Banks also deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) if your interest from that bank goes above ₹50,000 in a financial year (₹1 lakh for senior citizens).
Example: Dia puts ₹1,00,000 into a 3-year bank FD at 7% a year. She knows for certain that she will get back her ₹1,00,000 plus all the promised interest when the FD matures.

LEARNING POINT 2: Government & Corporate Bonds
When governments or companies need large amounts of money to build highways, power grids or factories, they borrow it from the public by issuing bonds. When you buy a bond, you become the lender: the issuer pays you regular interest (called coupon payments) and returns your full principal on the maturity date. Government bonds (G-Secs) are the safest and currently pay around 7% to 7.5% a year. Bonds issued by private companies carry more risk that the company can't pay you back, so they pay higher interest — the riskier the company, the more it has to offer.
Example: CA Jay helps Maanu put part of her savings into a government bond paying 7.3% a year, so she receives a steady interest payment into her bank account every six months.

LEARNING POINT 3: Bond Prices and Interest Rates — The Seesaw Rule
When bonds are bought and sold in the market, their prices move in the opposite direction to interest rates. When the central bank raises interest rates, new bonds pay more, so older bonds with lower rates become less attractive and their prices fall. When interest rates drop, older bonds paying higher rates become more valuable and their prices rise. Knowing this seesaw rule helps you avoid surprises if you ever sell a bond before it matures.
Example: Rudy holds an older bond paying 7%. If new bonds start paying 9%, buyers will only buy Rudy's bond at a discount, because they could earn more from a new one.

## Real-Life Fact
In India, bank fixed deposits are insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India — giving ordinary savers a high level of safety.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing that fixed deposit interest is tax-free and that bond prices never change after they are issued.
Correction: FD interest is fully taxable at your income tax slab rate, and bond prices rise and fall in the market whenever interest rates change.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Fixed deposits and bonds give steady, predictable income and bring balance and safety to your investments.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. What happens when you open a bank Fixed Deposit (FD)?
A. You buy shares of a private tech startup
B. You lock a sum of money with a bank for a fixed period at a guaranteed interest rate
C. You turn your cash into foreign lottery tickets
D. You lend money to strangers without any paperwork
Hint: Your money is placed for a set period with fixed returns.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: A Fixed Deposit locks a sum of money for an agreed period at a fixed, guaranteed interest rate.

Q2. What extra interest do senior citizens typically get on bank FDs in India?
A. 5% extra
B. About 0.5% extra
C. Zero interest
D. Double the principal every week
Hint: Banks give a small bonus (around half a percent) to people aged 60 and above.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Indian banks usually offer senior citizens about 0.5% (50 basis points) more interest on fixed deposits.

Q3. What is the key difference between a government bond and a corporate bond?
A. Government bonds are illegal
B. Government bonds are backed by the government and are safer, while corporate bonds pay more interest to make up for the company's risk
C. Corporate bonds are only issued by schools
D. Government bonds never pay any interest
Hint: Think about safety versus reward.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Government bonds have the lowest risk of not being repaid, so corporate bonds must pay higher interest to attract investors.

Q4. What happens to bond prices when interest rates in the economy go UP?
A. Bond prices shoot up to record highs
B. Bond prices go down, because older bonds paying lower rates become less attractive
C. Bond prices freeze and disappear
D. Bond prices are not affected by interest rates
Hint: Think of a seesaw — when interest rates rise, existing bond prices fall.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions: when rates rise, existing bond prices fall.

Q5. Above what amount of yearly FD interest from one bank does that bank deduct TDS for a regular (non-senior) individual in India?
A. ₹1,000 a year
B. ₹50,000 a year
C. ₹10 lakh a year
D. ₹1 crore a year
Hint: It is a five-figure amount. Senior citizens get double this limit.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Since April 2025, banks deduct TDS only when a regular individual's total FD interest from that bank in a financial year goes above ₹50,000 (₹1 lakh for senior citizens).

## Explore Further
Q1. What is the "coupon rate" of a bond?
A. A discount voucher used at supermarkets
B. The fixed yearly interest rate paid on the bond's face value
C. The penalty charged for selling a bond early
D. The tax deduction claimed on shares
Hint: It is the stated interest rate the issuer pays the bondholder.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The coupon rate is the yearly interest rate the issuer pays, calculated on the bond's face (par) value.

Q2. Which measure shows a bond's total expected yearly return if you hold it until it matures?
A. Price-to-Earnings Ratio
B. Yield to Maturity (YTM)
C. Dividend Payout Ratio
D. Gross Margin
Hint: Look for the yield calculated right to the end of the bond's life.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Yield to Maturity (YTM) is the yearly return an investor earns by holding the bond to maturity, assuming every coupon payment is reinvested at that same yield.

Q3. Which credit rating shows the highest safety and lowest risk of default for corporate bonds in India?
A. AAA
B. BBB-
C. D
D. Junk status
Hint: The top rating uses the same letter three times.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: A AAA rating from a SEBI-registered rating agency (such as CRISIL, ICRA or CARE) shows the highest safety and lowest risk of default.

Q4. What is a "zero-coupon bond"?
A. A bond that never pays back its principal
B. A bond sold at a big discount to its face value that pays no regular interest but pays the full face value at maturity
C. A bond that gives shopping coupons instead of cash
D. A bond issued only by charities
Hint: It pays zero regular coupons and earns its return through the discounted purchase price.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Zero-coupon bonds make no regular interest payments; they are sold at a discount and pay their full face value when they mature.

Q5. What is "duration risk" in a bond portfolio?
A. The risk of losing paper certificates
B. How sensitive a bond's price is to changes in interest rates
C. The time it takes for a bank cheque to clear
D. The risk that an investor forgets their password
Hint: Longer-term bonds swing more in price when interest rates change.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Duration measures how much a bond's price is likely to change, in percentage terms, when interest rates move by 1%. The longer the duration, the bigger the swing.
