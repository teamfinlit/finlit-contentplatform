## Quick Spark
A share of stock is a tiny slice of ownership in a real business. India's main stock exchanges, the NSE and BSE, are open for trading from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM on weekdays. Indices like the NIFTY 50 and SENSEX show how India's top companies are doing overall. In the short term prices move with supply and demand, but over the long term they follow how much profit the business makes!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: The Stock Market & Stock Ownership
A share of stock is a real slice of ownership in a company. When a company wants to grow, it can divide its ownership into millions of identical shares and list them so the public can buy and sell them. When you buy even one share of a company like Reliance Industries or Infosys, you become a part-owner (a shareholder) with a claim on a matching share of the company's future growth, assets and profits.
Example: Rudy and Maanu start a small lemonade stand. If they split its ownership into 10 paper certificates and Rudy buys 5, he owns 50% of the stand and gets half of all its future profits.

LEARNING POINT 2: Major Exchanges: NSE and BSE & Market Hours
Stock exchanges are tightly regulated digital marketplaces where investors can buy and sell shares safely. India has two main exchanges: the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), the oldest exchange in Asia, and the National Stock Exchange (NSE), the largest by trading volume. Normal trading runs from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM, Monday to Friday, except on market holidays. (Since August 2026, the shares of the biggest companies — those also traded in futures and options — stop normal trading at 3:15 PM and finish the day with a short closing auction that ends at 3:35 PM.)
Example: When Dia wants to buy shares of an Indian IT company on a Tuesday morning, her online order goes to the NSE and is completed during market hours.

LEARNING POINT 3: Benchmark Indices: NIFTY 50 and SENSEX
Because thousands of shares trade every day, indices act like a thermometer that shows the market's overall direction at a glance. The BSE SENSEX tracks 30 of the largest, strongest companies, while the NSE NIFTY 50 tracks 50 leading companies across many industries. If the NIFTY 50 rises 1% in a day, it means India's biggest companies, taken together, went up in value.
Example: CA Jay watches the evening news. When the anchor says the SENSEX rose 400 points, he knows India's top 30 companies had a good day overall.

LEARNING POINT 4: How Prices Move: Supply, Demand & Long-Term Earnings
In the short term, share prices change all day based on supply and demand. If many buyers want a share (high demand) and few people want to sell (low supply), the price goes up; if bad news makes lots of people sell, the price falls. Over five to ten years, though, these daily emotions settle down, and share prices tend to follow how much the business actually earns and how fast its profits grow.
Example: A bakery invents a hugely popular new biscuit, and its sales and profits double. More investors want its shares, and the share price climbs steadily over several years.

## Real-Life Fact
The Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) on Dalal Street in Mumbai was formally set up in 1875 and is Asia's first stock exchange. It grew out of a group of traders, including the merchant Premchand Roychand, who used to meet under a banyan tree.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing the stock market is a lottery where prices jump around at random with no link to the real world.
Correction: Day-to-day moves reflect traders' short-term moods, but over the long term share prices are tied to companies' real sales, profits and the strength of the economy.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Owning a share makes you a business owner, and strong company profits build lasting wealth over time.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. What does buying one share of a listed company mean?
A. You are lending money that must be paid back with interest tomorrow
B. You own a tiny part of that company
C. You become responsible for cleaning the company's offices
D. You get the company's products free for life
Hint: Think about what it means to be a shareholder and part-owner.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Buying a share gives you part-ownership of the business, with a claim on a matching share of its assets and profits.

Q2. What are the normal trading hours of the Indian stock market (NSE and BSE) on weekdays?
A. 24 hours a day, 7 days a week
B. 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM (Monday to Friday)
C. 6:00 PM to midnight
D. 12:00 PM to 1:00 PM only
Hint: Trading happens during morning and afternoon business hours.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Normal trading on the NSE and BSE runs from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM, Monday to Friday, except on market holidays. Since August 2026, the largest companies' shares finish with a short closing auction between 3:15 and 3:35 PM.

Q3. How many large Indian companies does the BSE SENSEX track?
A. 50 companies
B. 30 companies
C. 500 companies
D. 10 companies
Hint: SENSEX tracks 30 big companies, while NIFTY tracks 50.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: The BSE SENSEX is an index of 30 of the largest and most actively traded companies on the BSE, weighted by their market value.

Q4. If a company announces record profits and exciting new products, what usually happens to its share price?
A. The price drops to zero
B. More people want to buy than sell, so the price rises
C. The government stops trading in the share
D. The price freezes forever
Hint: When many buyers chase a limited number of shares, the price goes up.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Good results attract more buyers than sellers, which pushes the share price up.

Q5. What mostly decides where a share price goes over many years?
A. The colour and logo of the share certificate
B. The company's real earnings, sales growth and profits
C. Daily gossip in social media groups
D. The number of banknotes printed by banks
Hint: Over long periods, share prices reflect what the company actually earns.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: In the short run, emotions drive price swings, but over many years share prices tend to follow the company's real profits and cash flows.

## Explore Further
Q1. What is an Initial Public Offering (IPO)?
A. A yearly company holiday party
B. The process by which a private company sells its shares to the public for the first time to raise money
C. A penalty fee charged by central banks
D. Insurance against falling share prices
Hint: It is the first time a private company sells shares to ordinary investors.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: An IPO is when an unlisted company first offers its shares to institutional and retail investors to raise capital.

Q2. Which body is responsible for protecting investors and regulating the securities market in India?
A. Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI)
B. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
C. Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT)
D. Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI)
Hint: Look for the regulator set up under the SEBI Act, 1992.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is the statutory regulator for capital markets, stock exchanges and investor protection in India.

Q3. What does "market capitalisation" (market cap) measure?
A. The floor space of a company's head office
B. The total market value of all of a company's shares (Total Shares × Current Share Price)
C. The cash kept in the company's cash registers
D. The total yearly tax the company pays
Hint: Multiply the total number of shares by the current price of one share.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Market cap is the share price multiplied by the total number of shares, and it is used to group companies as large-cap, mid-cap or small-cap.

Q4. What is the difference between the "primary market" and the "secondary market"?
A. The primary market is for used goods and the secondary market is for raw materials
B. In the primary market, investors buy new shares directly from the company (for example in an IPO); in the secondary market, investors trade existing shares with each other (on the NSE/BSE)
C. Primary markets exist only on paper and secondary markets only on phones
D. Primary markets open only at weekends
Hint: The primary market is where new shares are born; the secondary market is where existing shares change hands.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The primary market handles new share issues between companies and investors, while the secondary market lets existing shareholders trade with each other.

Q5. What settlement cycle do Indian stock exchanges use to transfer shares and money after a trade?
A. T+30 (30 days later)
B. T+1 (one working day after the trade), with optional same-day (T+0) settlement available for some shares
C. T+365 (one year later)
D. Trades are never settled
Hint: India moved to a fast, one-day settlement cycle.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Indian exchanges settle trades on T+1 (the trade day plus one working day), and an optional same-day T+0 cycle is being rolled out for a limited set of shares.
