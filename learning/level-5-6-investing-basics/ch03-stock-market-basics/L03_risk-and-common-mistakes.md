## Quick Spark
Stock market risk includes the whole market falling and individual companies failing. Short-term ups and downs are normal, but panic selling during a dip turns a temporary loss into a real one. Spreading your money across different industries stops one bad company from sinking your whole portfolio. Avoid social media tips, over-trading and trying to time the market — time in the market wins!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: Stock Market Risks: Market Risk, Company Risk & Volatility
Investing in shares means your money is genuinely at risk, and every beginner should respect that. Market risk is when the whole economy is hit (such as the 2008 global financial crisis or the 2020 pandemic crash) and almost all shares fall together for a while. Company risk is a problem with one particular business — poor management, fraud in its accounts or out-of-date technology — which can make that one share lose value, or even become worthless, while the rest of the economy is doing well. Volatility means the sharp up-and-down price swings that happen every day; treating them as a normal part of investing, rather than as a permanent loss, is the key to staying calm.
Example: In early 2020, share prices around the world fell sharply within a few weeks because of market risk, yet strong companies recovered and reached new record highs over the next two years.

LEARNING POINT 2: Diversification (The Essential Shield)
The best way to reduce company risk is diversification: spreading your money across around 15 to 25 good companies in different industries (such as banking, technology, healthcare and consumer goods). If one company runs into trouble, the steady growth of the others cushions your overall portfolio. Putting all your savings into one exciting share is a dangerous gamble.
Example: Rudy puts all his pocket money into one toy company that suddenly shuts down, and he loses everything. Maanu spreads her money across five different businesses, so when one struggles, the other four protect her overall savings.

LEARNING POINT 3: Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid
New investors often hurt their returns by falling into the same traps. First, acting on unchecked "hot tips" from social media, WhatsApp groups or friends often leads to buying manipulated, low-quality penny stocks. Second, panic selling — dumping good investments in fear during a temporary fall — turns a loss on paper into a real, permanent loss. Third, over-trading (buying and selling many times a week) mostly benefits brokers through fees and taxes and eats away at your returns. Finally, trying to time the market by guessing the exact highs and lows rarely works; staying invested in good businesses for a long time and letting them compound works far better.
Example: CA Jay tells Maanu that investing is like growing an apple orchard: you water the trees patiently for years — you don't dig up the seeds every morning to check whether they have grown.

## Real-Life Fact
During the March 2020 crash, the NIFTY 50 fell nearly 38% in less than two months as panic spread. Investors who stayed calm and didn't sell saw the index more than double from its low, reaching new all-time highs within about 18 months.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing that investing in shares means predicting daily price moves and trading in and out on hot tips every morning.
Correction: Frequent trading brings high brokerage fees, taxes and stress. Long-term wealth is built by patiently holding a diversified basket of good businesses.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Time in the market beats timing the market — diversify your investments, ignore the noise, and never panic-sell during a temporary fall.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. What is "company risk" in investing?
A. The risk that the whole world economy shuts down forever
B. The danger that one particular business fails or struggles because of poor management, competition or debt
C. The fee the post office charges to send letters
D. The risk that banknotes change colour
Hint: This risk affects only one company, not the whole economy.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Company risk comes from problems unique to one business that can damage its profits and its share price.

Q2. What is "diversification"?
A. Putting 100% of your money into one risky share
B. Spreading your money across many companies and different industries to lower your overall risk
C. Checking your phone balance every five minutes
D. Borrowing money to buy expensive cars
Hint: Don't put all your eggs in one basket.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Diversification lowers both the ups and downs of a portfolio and company risk by spreading money across different industries and types of investment.

Q3. Why is acting on unasked-for "stock tips" from WhatsApp or social media dangerous?
A. Social media apps delete your bank account automatically
B. Tips often push low-quality shares so that manipulators can sell their own shares to unsuspecting buyers
C. Central banks stop phones from using the internet
D. Stock tips are written in secret code
Hint: Free tips are often used to manipulate rarely traded penny stocks.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Unregulated tips on social media are often part of "pump and dump" schemes designed to trap inexperienced investors.

Q4. What is the main problem with "over-trading" (buying and selling shares very often)?
A. It drains your computer's battery
B. It adds up to high brokerage fees, taxes and stress, while lowering long-term returns
C. It doubles your money every hour
D. It makes companies shut their factories
Hint: Every extra trade costs money and adds to emotional mistakes.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Trading too often adds costs, short-term capital gains tax and emotional mistakes, all of which reduce your final returns.

Q5. What does "Time in the market beats timing the market" mean?
A. You must watch the share prices 24 hours a day
B. Holding good, diversified investments for many years builds far more wealth than trying to guess short-term highs and lows
C. You should sell all your investments every Friday afternoon
D. Markets only reward people who trade in less than a second
Hint: Holding patiently lets compounding work without guessing daily swings.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Capturing years of compounding from good businesses gives better results than trying to time the market's ups and downs.

## Explore Further
Q1. What is the difference between "systematic risk" and "unsystematic risk"?
A. Systematic risk affects the whole market and can't be removed by diversifying; unsystematic risk is specific to a company and can be reduced by diversifying
B. Systematic risk applies only to gold, and unsystematic risk only to bank accounts
C. Systematic risk is illegal in financial markets
D. There is no difference between them
Hint: Systematic means market-wide; unsystematic means company-specific.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Systematic risk (such as recessions or interest rate changes) affects all investments, while unsystematic risk (specific to one business) can be largely removed by diversifying.

Q2. Which measure shows how much a share's price tends to move compared with the overall market?
A. Alpha
B. Beta (β)
C. Delta
D. Theta
Hint: A value above 1 means the share swings more than the market index.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Beta (β) measures how much a share's returns move compared with a benchmark index, where the market itself has a beta of 1.0.

Q3. Which bias makes people feel the pain of losing money about twice as strongly as the joy of gaining the same amount?
A. Overconfidence bias
B. Loss aversion (prospect theory)
C. Anchoring
D. Gambler's fallacy
Hint: It was identified by the psychologists Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Loss aversion means the pain of losing money feels roughly twice as strong as the pleasure of gaining the same amount.

Q4. What is a "pump and dump" scheme?
A. Building a water tank near a factory
B. Fraudsters hype up a rarely traded share with false claims to push its price up, then sell their own shares for a profit, leaving other buyers with a crashing price
C. Keeping emergency cash in several bank accounts
D. A central bank operation to print money
Hint: The price is "pumped" up artificially, then the shares are "dumped" on other investors.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: In a pump and dump, fraudsters push up the price of a thinly traded share with misleading promotion, then sell their holdings, causing heavy losses for the people who bought late.

Q5. What is a "market drawdown", and how should a long-term investor respond?
A. The stock market disappears forever, so investors should give up saving
B. A fall from a previous peak to a low point; disciplined investors stick to their long-term plan and keep investing regularly instead of panic-selling
C. A government order to pay extra penalties on every share owned
D. A forced conversion of all shares into gold bars
Hint: Drawdowns are normal market falls that can be buying opportunities for long-term investors.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Drawdowns are regular falls from earlier market highs. Long-term investors treat them as part of the market cycle and avoid locking in losses by panic-selling.
