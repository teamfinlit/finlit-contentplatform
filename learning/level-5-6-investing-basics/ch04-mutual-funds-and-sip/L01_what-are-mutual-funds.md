## Quick Spark
A mutual fund pools money from thousands of ordinary investors to buy a wide mix of shares or bonds. Professional fund managers decide what to buy and sell, under strict SEBI rules that protect investors. Net Asset Value (NAV) is the daily price of one unit of the fund. Funds range from safer debt funds and steady large-cap funds to faster-growing mid- and small-cap funds and low-cost index funds!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: Mutual Funds Explained & SEBI Oversight
Instead of researching and buying dozens of company shares yourself, you can put your money into a mutual fund, which collects money from thousands of ordinary savers into one big shared pool. A full-time professional fund manager uses this pool to build a diversified portfolio of shares, government securities or corporate bonds. In India, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) closely regulates every mutual fund company (called an Asset Management Company, or AMC) to protect investors from fraud and make sure everything is transparent.
Example: Rudy and 40 classmates each put in ₹50 to buy a huge art set with 100 premium colours that none of them could afford alone, and they ask their art teacher to look after it.

LEARNING POINT 2: Net Asset Value (NAV)
When you invest in a mutual fund, you buy units of that fund. The Net Asset Value (NAV) is the value of one unit, worked out at the end of every trading day. It is calculated by adding up the market value of everything the fund owns, subtracting its expenses, and dividing by the total number of units held by investors.
Example: If a fund owns ₹10,00,000 worth of shares and has issued 50,000 units, each unit has an NAV of ₹20 (₹10,00,000 ÷ 50,000 = ₹20).

LEARNING POINT 3: Broad Categories: Equity, Debt & Hybrid Funds
Mutual funds are built around different types of assets to suit different goals. Equity funds invest in company shares for long-term growth. Debt funds invest in government bonds and corporate bonds to earn steady interest with smaller ups and downs. Hybrid funds hold both shares and bonds to balance growth with stability.
Example: Dia wants safety for next year's school fees, so she chooses a low-risk debt fund, while she invests in an equity fund for Rudy's college fees 10 years away.

LEARNING POINT 4: Company Size: Large Cap, Mid Cap & Small Cap
Equity funds are also grouped by the size of the companies they invest in. Large-cap funds invest in India's 100 biggest, most established companies and aim for steady returns. Mid-cap funds invest in medium-sized companies ranked 101 to 250 by market value, which can grow strongly but with more ups and downs. Small-cap funds invest in smaller, up-and-coming companies ranked 251 and below, which have the most growth potential but also swing the most, especially when the economy slows.
Example: Maanu puts most of her pocket-money savings into a large-cap fund for reliability and a smaller part into a small-cap fund for higher long-term growth.

LEARNING POINT 5: Index Funds (Low-Cost Passive Investing)
In an actively managed fund, a manager hand-picks shares to try to beat the market. An index fund simply copies a market index (like the NIFTY 50 or SENSEX), buying the same companies in the same proportions. Because it doesn't need an expensive research team or a manager's guesswork, an index fund charges very low fees and gives you the return of the overall market.
Example: CA Jay helps Dia set up an automatic investment in a low-cost NIFTY 50 index fund, so she owns a small piece of India's top 50 companies while paying very low fees.

## Real-Life Fact
According to the Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI), Indian mutual funds managed about ₹82.6 lakh crore of investors' money in September 2026, and Systematic Investment Plans brought in a record ₹32,490 crore that month alone.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing that investing in mutual funds is the same as buying lottery tickets or gambling on risky shares.
Correction: Mutual funds are professionally managed, regulated portfolios spread across dozens of real, audited companies and bonds, which greatly reduces the risk of one company's failure hurting you.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: A mutual fund gives you instant diversification and professional management with small, affordable amounts.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. What is a mutual fund?
A. A private bank vault that stores only gold coins
B. A pooled investment where money from many investors is managed professionally across shares or bonds
C. A government tax on shopping
D. A lottery ticket bought at a railway counter
Hint: Think about pooling money together for shared ownership.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: A mutual fund pools money from many investors to build a diversified portfolio managed by professionals.

Q2. What does Net Asset Value (NAV) represent?
A. The fund manager's total salary
B. The daily value of one unit of a mutual fund
C. The number of brochures printed
D. A penalty for opening an account
Hint: NAV is the price tag of one unit of the fund.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: NAV is the value of everything the fund owns, minus expenses, divided by the number of units, and it is updated every day.

Q3. Which body regulates and supervises all mutual funds in India?
A. Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI)
B. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
C. Food Corporation of India (FCI)
D. Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC)
Hint: Look for the national regulator of the investment markets.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) sets the rules, investment limits and disclosure standards for all mutual funds in India.

Q4. Under SEBI's classification, which companies do large-cap funds mainly invest in?
A. Unregistered corner shops
B. The 100 largest listed companies by market value
C. Companies ranked 500 to 1,000
D. Foreign start-ups with no sales
Hint: Large-caps are the 100 biggest companies in the country.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: SEBI defines large-cap companies as the top 100 listed companies by full market capitalisation.

Q5. What is the main advantage of a passive index fund?
A. It guarantees a profit every single day
B. It copies a market index at very low cost and removes the risk of a manager's poor choices
C. It removes all taxes forever
D. It is run by artificial intelligence with no government rules
Hint: Index funds copy a benchmark automatically and charge tiny fees.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Index funds simply track a market index, so they avoid high management fees and stock-picking mistakes while matching the market's return.

## Explore Further
Q1. In a mutual fund, who holds the fund's assets on behalf of investors and checks that the fund company follows the rules?
A. The marketing agency
B. The Board of Trustees
C. The stockbroker
D. The local police department
Hint: Trustees hold the assets "in trust" for the unitholders.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Board of Trustees holds the fund's assets in trust for the unitholders and makes sure the AMC follows SEBI's regulations.

Q2. What is an "open-ended" mutual fund, compared with a "closed-ended" one?
A. Open-ended funds let you buy and sell units on any business day at that day's NAV, while closed-ended funds have a fixed maturity date
B. Open-ended funds charge no expenses
C. Closed-ended funds are completely unregulated
D. Open-ended funds can only hold silver bars
Hint: Open-ended funds let you invest or withdraw all year round.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Open-ended funds accept new money and redemptions at the current NAV on any business day and have no fixed maturity date.

Q3. How does SEBI define mid-cap and small-cap companies by market ranking?
A. Mid-cap: 1st to 50th; small-cap: 51st to 100th
B. Mid-cap: 101st to 250th; small-cap: 251st and below, by market value
C. Mid-cap: companies with 10 employees; small-cap: companies with 2 employees
D. Mid-cap: property only; small-cap: gold only
Hint: Mid-caps are ranked 101–250, and small-caps are everything from 251 onwards.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Under SEBI's rules, mid-cap companies are ranked 101st to 250th by market capitalisation, and small-cap companies are ranked 251st and below.

Q4. What does a "liquid fund" do within the debt fund category?
A. It invests in overseas oil companies
B. It invests in very short-term money market instruments maturing within 91 days, keeping money safe and easy to withdraw
C. It buys high-risk small-cap tech shares
D. It funds 30-year building projects
Hint: Liquid funds are a safe parking place for short-term cash.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Liquid funds invest in debt and money market instruments that mature within 91 days, so they have low interest-rate risk and you can withdraw quickly.

Q5. What is "tracking error" in index fund investing?
A. A mistake made by a bank when transferring cash
B. A measure of how much an index fund's returns differ from the returns of the index it copies, over a year
C. The time it takes to post account statements
D. A penalty for selling units
Hint: It shows how closely the index fund follows its target index.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Tracking error is the yearly variation (standard deviation) in the gap between an index fund's returns and its index's returns. The lower it is, the more closely the fund follows the index.
