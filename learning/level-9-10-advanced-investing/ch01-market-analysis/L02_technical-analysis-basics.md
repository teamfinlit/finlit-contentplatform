## Quick Spark
Technical analysis studies price charts, trading volume, and momentum indicators to evaluate market trends. Support acts as a price floor where buyers step in; resistance acts as a price ceiling where sellers emerge. Moving averages smooth out daily price noise, with a Golden Cross widely read as a bullish trend signal. Disciplined investors use fundamental analysis to select great businesses and technicals to help with entry timing!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: Technical Analysis & Reading Candlestick Charts
While fundamental analysis evaluates what business to own, technical analysis evaluates when market participants are actively buying or selling by examining historical price action and trading volume patterns. Modern traders visualize this using Candlestick Charts. Each individual candle encapsulates four key price points across a timeframe: Open, High, Low, and Close (OHLC). A Green (bullish) candlestick indicates that buyers pushed the closing price higher than the opening price, while a Red (bearish) candlestick shows that sellers drove the closing price below the open.
Example: Rudy looks at a daily chart of a stock; a long green candlestick with a short wick shows that buyers dominated the entire trading session from morning to evening.

LEARNING POINT 2: Support, Resistance & Market Trends
Market prices move in trends: Uptrends (higher highs and higher lows), Downtrends (lower highs and lower lows), or Sideways consolidations. Two critical horizontal boundaries govern price flow: Support is a price level where buying pressure has historically been strong enough to halt further declines and bounce prices upward (like a solid rubber ball bouncing off a concrete floor); Resistance is a price ceiling where selling pressure overpowers demand, stopping upward rallies and pushing prices back down.
Example: Every time an automobile company's stock drops to ₹500, a wave of buyers steps in to lift it back up (Support); but whenever it climbs to ₹650, sellers take profits and prevent it from climbing higher (Resistance).

LEARNING POINT 3: Moving Averages & The Golden Cross
Moving Averages (like the 50-day and 200-day Simple Moving Averages) calculate the average closing price over set lookback periods, smoothing out erratic daily noise to reveal the underlying trend direction. A widely watched technical milestone is the Golden Cross, which occurs when a short-term moving average (such as the 50-day MA) crosses decisively above a long-term moving average (such as the 200-day MA). Traders read this as a sign of shifting momentum and a possible longer-term uptrend, though like every technical signal it can also give false alarms.
Example: CA Jay shows Maanu a stock chart where the fast 50-day line climbs sharply above the slow 200-day baseline, which many traders read as a sign that buying momentum is building.

LEARNING POINT 4: Volume, RSI & MACD Momentum Indicators
Trading Volume represents the total number of shares traded in a session; price breakouts accompanied by unusually high volume suggest strong conviction among buyers, whereas low-volume price moves often prove to be false traps. Momentum oscillators provide mathematical checks on market extremes: the Relative Strength Index (RSI) measures the speed of price changes on a 0–100 scale, where an RSI above 70 indicates an overbought condition (potential pullback) and an RSI below 30 indicates an oversold level (potential recovery). The Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) tracks the relationship between two exponential moving averages to generate trend confirmation and momentum signals.
Example: Maanu spots a stock breaking above resistance; because the breakout is backed by huge trading volume and an MACD positive crossover while the RSI is at a healthy 55, technical analysts consider it a validated momentum move.

## Real-Life Fact
Japanese candlestick charting is traditionally credited to the 18th-century rice merchant Munehisa Homma, who traded at the Dojima Rice Exchange in Osaka and observed that market prices were heavily driven by the emotions of fear and greed, not just by the supply of rice.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Relying 100% on technical chart patterns while completely ignoring whether the underlying company actually makes profits or carries massive unpayable debts.
Correction: Technical indicators help time entries and assess supply-demand momentum, but long-term investment success requires backing by solid fundamental business earnings and balance sheet safety.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Use fundamental analysis to select the right business to own, and use technical analysis to understand market momentum and entry timing.

## Check Your Thinking
Q1. What is the primary difference between a green candlestick and a red candlestick on a price chart?
A. Green means the company changed its name; Red means the company went bankrupt
B. Green indicates the price closed higher than it opened (bullish); Red indicates the price closed lower than it opened (bearish)
C. Green is used only on Mondays; Red is used only on Fridays
D. Green is for gold; Red is for silver
Hint: Green represents upward price progress; Red represents downward price pressure.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: A green (bullish) candlestick indicates positive session performance (Close > Open), while a red (bearish) candle reflects negative performance (Close < Open).

Q2. What does a "Support Level" represent in technical market analysis?
A. A price ceiling where everyone sells all shares
B. A price floor where historical buying demand is strong enough to halt downward drops and trigger rebounds
C. The total amount of tax paid to the government
D. A bank account opening fee
Hint: Think of a floor that supports falling prices from dropping lower.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Support is a horizontal price level where buying interest matches and exceeds selling pressure, stopping the downward price move.

Q3. What technical event defines a classic "Golden Cross"?
A. The price of gold reaches an all-time record high
B. A short-term moving average (e.g., 50-day MA) crosses decisively above a long-term moving average (e.g., 200-day MA)
C. A company declares a 100% dividend payout
D. All stock markets close down for a holiday
Hint: A faster-moving average line crosses above a slower-moving baseline.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: A Golden Cross occurs when a short-term moving average crosses above a long-term moving average, which is widely read as a bullish trend signal.

Q4. On the Relative Strength Index (RSI) scale of 0 to 100, what do readings above 70 and below 30 typically signal?
A. Above 70 = Oversold; Below 30 = Overbought
B. Above 70 = Overbought (extended upside); Below 30 = Oversold (extended downside)
C. Both indicate that the company has stopped operating
D. RSI numbers measure the temperature inside stock exchanges
Hint: High numbers reflect heated buying; low numbers reflect heavy selling exhaustion.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: Standard RSI settings treat values above 70 as overbought conditions prone to pullbacks, and below 30 as oversold zones prone to rebounds.

Q5. Why is high trading volume crucial when evaluating a price breakout above resistance?
A. It makes stock certificates heavier to carry
B. It confirms strong participation and conviction behind the price move, reducing the risk of a false breakout
C. It automatically eliminates all capital gains taxes
D. It forces central banks to cut interest rates
Hint: Large volume shows major market players are backing the price move.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: A price breakout backed by heavy volume shows widespread market commitment to the new price level.

## Explore Further
Q1. What technical pattern is characterized by a high peak flanked by two lower peaks, signaling a potential trend reversal from bullish to bearish?
A. Cup and Handle
B. Head and Shoulders
C. Ascending Triangle
D. Bull Flag
Hint: The central peak resembles a head surrounded by two left and right shoulders.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Head and Shoulders pattern features a left shoulder, higher head, and right shoulder with a baseline "neckline," signaling a topping reversal.

Q2. What is the mathematical difference between a Simple Moving Average (SMA) and an Exponential Moving Average (EMA)?
A. An EMA assigns higher weighting to recent price data, making it react faster to new price changes than an SMA
B. An SMA is only used for cryptocurrency assets
C. An EMA ignores all closing prices
D. There is zero mathematical difference
Hint: Exponential calculations weight the latest data points more heavily.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: EMAs apply an exponential weighting multiplier that emphasizes recent price action, reducing lag compared to arithmetic Simple Moving Averages.

Q3. What is "Bullish Divergence" when analyzing price charts with the RSI or MACD oscillator?
A. Price makes a new lower low while the oscillator makes a higher low, signaling weakening downward momentum and a possible upward reversal
B. Price doubles every single week without volume
C. All market trading stops for 24 hours
D. Stock price moves in exact lockstep with company revenue
Hint: The price falls to new lows, but the momentum indicator refuses to make a lower low.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Bullish divergence occurs when asset prices hit lower lows while momentum indicators form higher lows, indicating exhaustion of selling pressure.

Q4. What is the "Death Cross" in technical chart analysis?
A. A permanent shutdown of a stock exchange
B. A bearish technical signal where the 50-day moving average crosses below the 200-day moving average
C. An error in a broker's electronic database
D. A tax penalty on inherited shares
Hint: It is the exact bearish opposite of a Golden Cross.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: A Death Cross is a recognized technical pattern where a short-term moving average falls below a long-term moving average, indicating bearish momentum.

Q5. What does the "Average True Range" (ATR) indicator quantify on a technical price chart?
A. The total corporate dividend payout ratio
B. Market volatility, by measuring the average range between session highs and lows over a specified timeframe
C. The exact future stock price with 100% certainty
D. The book value per share of an enterprise
Hint: Developed by J. Welles Wilder Jr. to measure volatility independent of trend direction.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Average True Range (ATR) measures market volatility by averaging the full price range (including gaps from the previous close) over a set period, which helps traders size stop-losses.
