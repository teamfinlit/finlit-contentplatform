# Case: ₹10k SIP for 20 Years - Index vs Active
**Level:** 9-10 Advanced Investing | **Chapter:** 1 - Market Analysis | **Topic:** 6

## Key Concepts
- ₹10,000 SIP comparison
- Fee drag
- NIFTY 50 index vs average large-cap active fund

## Quick Spark
A ₹10,000 monthly SIP running for 20 years creates life-changing compounding wealth. A low-cost NIFTY 50 Index Fund earning 12% a year grows it to about ₹1.00 crore (₹99.9 lakh). An average active large-cap fund earning the same market return but paying 1.5% a year in extra fees reaches only about ₹81.8 lakh. That fee gap costs you about ₹18.2 lakh — control your investment costs, because fees compound too!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: The Real Numbers: The ₹10,000 Monthly SIP Over 20 Years
Let us examine a worked comparison that reveals the true cost of investment fees. Suppose you invest ₹10,000 every single month through an automated Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) for exactly 20 years. Over these 240 months, your total out-of-pocket contribution is ₹24,00,000 (₹24 lakh). Let us compare where that money ends up when placed into a low-cost Index Fund versus an average actively managed Large-Cap fund over the same two decades.
Example: Rudy and Maanu both commit to investing ₹10,000 from their monthly salaries for 20 years; Rudy picks a passive NIFTY 50 Index Fund while Maanu picks an actively managed Large-Cap equity fund.

LEARNING POINT 2: Outcome in a Low-Cost NIFTY 50 Index Fund (~₹1.00 Crore)
A passive NIFTY 50 Index Fund has very low running costs (charging about 0.1% to 0.2% in fees). Assuming it delivers a net return of about 12.0% a year over the 20 years, your ₹10,000 monthly SIP compounds into ₹99,91,479 (about ₹1.00 crore). Compounding contributed nearly ₹76 lakh in pure growth on top of your ₹24 lakh principal!
Example: Rudy opens his index portfolio after 20 years and discovers his ₹24 lakh of disciplined deposits has compounded into an entire ₹1 crore nest egg.

LEARNING POINT 3: Outcome in an Average Active Large-Cap Fund (~₹81.8 Lakh)
An actively managed Large-Cap equity fund must pay salaries, research teams, marketing, and distributor commissions, so it charges an annual Expense Ratio of around 1.5% to 1.8%. Even if the active fund manager matches the market's return, an extra 1.5% a year in fees reduces the investor's net return to 10.5% a year. At 10.5%, the exact same ₹10,000 monthly SIP compounds to only ₹81,75,968 (about ₹81.8 lakh).
Example: Maanu opens her active fund portfolio after 20 years; although she contributed the exact same ₹24 lakh as Rudy, her balance stands at about ₹81.8 lakh.

LEARNING POINT 4: The ₹18.2 Lakh Difference: The Compounding Cost of Fees
The difference between the two portfolios is an eye-opening ₹18.2 lakh (₹99.9 lakh − ₹81.8 lakh). That ₹18.2 lakh did not vanish through bad luck; it was steadily drained away by the extra 1.5% annual fee compounding against you every single day. In fact, that lost ₹18.2 lakh is more than 75% of your entire original investment (₹24 lakh).
Example: CA Jay shows Dia that a seemingly tiny 1.5% fee difference is large enough to buy a brand-new family car or fund several years of university tuition.

LEARNING POINT 5: Focus on What You Can Control: Controlling Investment Costs
In the financial markets, you can never predict or control what next year's GDP growth will be, which political party will win elections, or how stock prices will swing tomorrow. However, you have full control over the fees you pay and the discipline with which you invest. By choosing low-cost index funds and direct mutual fund plans, you make sure that most of the market's compounding stays in your pocket rather than going to fund houses.
Example: Dia switches all the family's long-term equity SIPs to direct index funds, locking in the lowest possible costs and maximizing the wealth passed down to Rudy and Maanu.

## Real-Life Fact
Over a 20-year monthly SIP, an extra 1.5% in annual fees (12% versus 10.5% a year) reduces the final amount by about 18% — proof that investment costs act as a powerful compounding headwind on long-term wealth.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing that a 1.5% annual expense ratio is a minor detail that won't make any noticeable difference to your life savings.
Correction: Because fees compound alongside returns over 20 to 30 years, a 1.5% fee gap silently drains lakhs of rupees (about ₹18.2 lakh on a ₹10,000 monthly SIP over 20 years) straight out of your pocket.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: You cannot control what the stock market will do tomorrow, but you can control the fees you pay — keep costs low and let your money work for you.
