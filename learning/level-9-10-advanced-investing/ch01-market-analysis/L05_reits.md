# REITs
**Level:** 9-10 Advanced Investing | **Chapter:** 1 - Market Analysis | **Topic:** 5

## Key Concepts
- Own commercial real estate
- From the price of one unit
- 90% distributable income
- Liquid
- SEBI regulation

## Quick Spark
Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) let you own institutional commercial properties for the price of a single unit. India's listed REITs include Embassy, Mindspace, Brookfield, Nexus Select and Knowledge Realty. By SEBI mandate, REITs must distribute at least 90% of their net distributable cash flow to unitholders. REITs trade publicly on stock exchanges, providing real estate income with stock-like liquidity!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: What Is a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT)?
Purchasing physical commercial properties—like multi-acre IT software parks, grade-A corporate towers, and shopping malls—typically requires hundreds of crores of rupees, putting them out of reach for individual savers. A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) solves this barrier by creating a structure that owns, manages, and operates a large portfolio of revenue-generating commercial real estate assets. Just like a mutual fund pools money to buy stocks, a REIT pools capital from thousands of public investors, divides ownership into tradable units, and lets everyday retail investors own a fractional slice of prime commercial buildings.
Example: Rudy and his friends want to own a bustling shopping complex; instead of needing ₹50 crore to buy the land, they buy 5 units of a publicly listed retail REIT for about ₹300 per unit, becoming genuine fractional landlords.

LEARNING POINT 2: Major Indian REITs: Office Parks & Retail Malls
India has five listed, SEBI-registered REITs spanning key commercial sectors. Office REITs like Embassy Office Parks REIT, Mindspace Business Parks REIT, Brookfield India Real Estate Trust and Knowledge Realty Trust own millions of square feet of tech hubs and corporate office parks leased to large multinational companies in cities such as Bengaluru, Mumbai, Pune, and Hyderabad. The retail REIT Nexus Select Trust owns high-footfall shopping malls and entertainment centers across major Indian cities.
Example: Dia visits a premier shopping mall or a major IT campus; because she owns units of Nexus Select and Embassy REIT, a portion of the rental revenue collected from the shops and tech tenants is distributed to her bank account.

LEARNING POINT 3: The 90% Cash Flow Distribution Rule (SEBI Mandate)
Under SEBI regulations, Indian REITs must distribute at least 90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unitholders at least once every six months (and most Indian REITs pay out every quarter). This rental income reaches investors as dividends, interest payouts, and return of capital, providing dependable, predictable cash flow.
Example: CA Jay shows Maanu his quarterly brokerage statement, where a steady cash distribution was credited automatically into his bank balance from commercial tenants paying their office rent.

LEARNING POINT 4: Dual Wealth Engines: Rental Yields + Capital Appreciation
REITs offer a dual wealth-building engine. First, they provide annual distribution yields — currently around 5% to 7% for Indian REITs — well above typical residential property rental yields in India (only about 2% to 4%). Second, as land values rise, new office towers are completed, and tenant lease contracts escalate over time, the Net Asset Value (NAV) of the underlying properties can increase, delivering long-term capital appreciation in the unit price.
Example: Maanu invests ₹10,000 in an Indian REIT; she collects about 6% a year in distributions while watching the market price of her REIT units rise over a 5-year horizon.

LEARNING POINT 5: Stock-Like Liquidity & Professional Management
Unlike physical property—which takes months to sell, involves hefty stamp duty and registration costs, requires dealing with brokers, and risks tenant default—REIT units trade openly on the NSE and BSE. You can buy or sell your REIT units with a single tap on your mobile trading app in seconds, with no property maintenance headaches or broker hassles.
Example: If Dia needs funds for an emergency, she can sell her REIT units on the stock exchange on a Tuesday morning and have the settled money in her bank account the next working day under T+1 settlement.

## Real-Life Fact
SEBI reduced the minimum application value and trading lot for REITs to just 1 unit, so retail investors can now start with the price of a single unit — roughly ₹110 to ₹510 across India's five listed REITs in late 2026 — opening up institutional commercial real estate to every investor.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Believing that investing in real estate always requires taking a huge 20-year bank loan to buy a physical residential apartment.
Correction: Residential apartments offer low rental yields (2%–4%) and very little liquidity; REITs let you start with the price of a single unit in commercial office towers and malls yielding around 5%–7%, with stock exchange liquidity.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: REITs combine the steady income of commercial real estate with the liquidity and low entry cost of the stock market.

## Explore Further
Q1. In real estate analytics, what does the "Weighted Average Lease Expiry" (WALE) of a REIT measure?
A. The average age of the elevators in the buildings
B. The average time remaining across all active tenant lease contracts, weighted by leased area or rental value
C. The time it takes to build a new parking lot
D. The retirement age of the REIT's CEO
Hint: A longer WALE indicates more visibility and predictability of future rental cash flows.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: WALE measures the average remaining duration of tenant leases weighted by occupied area or rent, signaling revenue stability and tenant retention strength.

Q2. What is an Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT) and how does it differ from a REIT?
A. InvITs only build residential bungalows
B. InvITs pool capital to own and operate infrastructure assets (such as toll roads, power transmission lines, and pipelines) rather than commercial buildings
C. InvITs are unregulated speculative tokens
D. InvITs can only be owned by foreign governments
Hint: InvITs focus on long-term national infrastructure assets like roads and power grids.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: InvITs are similar to REITs but own long-term infrastructure assets (highways, fibre networks, power grids) rather than commercial real estate.

Q3. What are the three components of a quarterly cash payout received from an Indian REIT, for tax purposes?
A. Gold tokens, equity shares, and shopping vouchers
B. Dividend, Interest, and Return of Capital (repayment of SPV debt)
C. Lottery prize, salary bonus, and insurance claim
D. Municipal tax, penalty charge, and broker fee
Hint: REIT payouts are broken down into interest, dividend, and principal return components.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: REIT distributions are split into dividend income, interest income, and return of capital (debt repayment from Special Purpose Vehicles), each with its own tax treatment.

Q4. What is the "Capitalization Rate" (Cap Rate) used in commercial property valuation?
A. The total number of shares issued by a bank
B. The ratio of Net Operating Income (NOI) to the property's current market value (NOI ÷ Current Value)
C. The interest rate charged on student loans
D. The speed of digital wire transfers
Hint: It shows the return a commercial property earns before any loan is taken into account.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The capitalization rate equals Net Operating Income divided by current property market value, measuring the property's basic return without debt financing.

Q5. What gives commercial office REITs built-in protection against inflation over long tenant leases?
A. The government fixes office rent at ₹10 forever
B. Contractual lease escalation clauses that automatically raise rents — typically around 10%–15% every 3 years
C. Tenants pay rent with physical silver bars
D. Central banks pay for all office utilities
Hint: Multi-year corporate leases include fixed rent increases.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Commercial leases usually include agreed escalation clauses (for example, a 12%–15% rent increase every 3 years), giving built-in revenue growth that helps offset inflation.
