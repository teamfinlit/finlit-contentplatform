# Index vs Active Funds
**Level:** 9-10 Advanced Investing | **Chapter:** 1 - Market Analysis | **Topic:** 3

## Key Concepts
- Benchmark tracking
- Active management
- Expense ratios
- SPIVA data
- Low-cost Index SIP

## Quick Spark
Index funds passively replicate market benchmarks, while active funds pay managers to pick winning stocks. Active funds charge significantly higher expense ratios (~1.5%) compared to ultra-low index funds (~0.2%). SPIVA data shows that most active large-cap funds fail to beat their index over the long run — about 3 in 4 in India over 10 years. Setting up an automated, low-cost Index Fund SIP is a powerful, evidence-backed wealth engine!

## Understand It
LEARNING POINT 1: The Index vs. Active Fund Architecture
In the investment world, two competing philosophies exist to grow capital. An Active Mutual Fund hires full-time fund managers, research analysts, and trading teams who actively buy, sell, and rotate stocks in an effort to "beat" the market index. Conversely, a Passive Index Fund abandons active guessing altogether: it simply replicates an official market benchmark (such as the NIFTY 50 or BSE SENSEX) by holding the exact same flagship companies in the exact same weights as the index.
Example: Rudy joins a relay team where a coach constantly swaps runners in and out hoping to run faster (Active), while Maanu joins a team that automatically runs with the proven top 50 athletes in the state every time (Index).

LEARNING POINT 2: The Massive Compounding Drag of Expense Ratios
Because active funds carry expensive salaries, administrative research overhead, and higher portfolio trading friction, they charge steep Expense Ratios—frequently around 1.0% to 1.8% annually. In contrast, passive Index Funds require no research teams and operate with razor-thin fees of 0.1% to 0.2%. While a 1.5% annual fee gap may sound trivial on day one, that difference compounds aggressively over time. Over a 20- to 30-year career, an extra 1.5% in ongoing management fees can consume roughly a quarter to a third of your total final portfolio wealth.
Example: Dia invests in an active fund charging 1.6% in fees while CA Jay invests in a NIFTY 50 Index fund charging 0.15%; after 25 years, CA Jay keeps lakhs of rupees more simply by eliminating unnecessary fee drag.

LEARNING POINT 3: The Cold Truth of SPIVA Data
The S&P Indices Versus Active (SPIVA) scorecard is the global gold standard that tracks the performance of active fund managers against their benchmarks over multi-year periods. The evidence is consistent: in India, the year-end 2025 SPIVA report found that about 76% of actively managed large-cap funds trailed their benchmark over 10 years (and about 84% over 5 years), and in the United States the share is even higher. Sustaining consistent market-beating alpha year after year is extremely difficult for most active managers.
Example: Maanu reviews a 10-year scorecard of 100 active large-cap fund managers and discovers that only about 24 of them delivered higher net returns than their benchmark index.

LEARNING POINT 4: The Low-Cost Index SIP: The Smart Default Strategy
For beginner and advanced investors alike, the most dependable, stress-free strategy is running an automated monthly SIP in a diversified, low-cost broad-market Index Fund. This approach captures the full market return of the nation's top corporations, eliminates single-manager stock-picking error, keeps transaction costs and fee leakage very low, and frees up your valuable time from constantly tracking fund manager changes.
Example: Maanu sets up an automated ₹2,000 monthly SIP into a NIFTY 50 Index Fund, confident that she is owning India's 50 largest companies at the lowest possible cost.

## Real-Life Fact
According to the SPIVA India Scorecard for year-end 2025, published by S&P Dow Jones Indices, 76.3% of actively managed Indian large-cap equity funds underperformed their benchmark over 10 years, and 84.4% did so over 5 years.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Assuming that paying higher management fees to an active fund manager guarantees superior investment returns.
Correction: In financial markets, higher fees directly reduce your net returns; extensive data shows that low-cost index funds beat the large majority of high-fee active funds over the long run.

## Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway: Minimize investment fees with low-cost Index Funds and let broad economic growth compound your wealth.

## Explore Further
Q1. What financial theory, formulated by Eugene Fama, says that asset prices reflect all available information, making it difficult to consistently achieve alpha through active stock picking?
A. The Austrian Business Cycle Theory
B. The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH)
C. The Quantity Theory of Money
D. The Marginal Utility Framework
Hint: Prices rapidly absorb public news and company data.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) states that share prices reflect all available information, meaning consistent risk-adjusted outperformance (alpha) is rare after fees.

Q2. What is "Alpha" versus "Beta" in investment portfolio performance metrics?
A. Alpha measures excess return generated above the benchmark; Beta measures broad market sensitivity and co-movement
B. Alpha is a type of corporate bond; Beta is a government savings certificate
C. Alpha measures annual tax rates; Beta measures inflation
D. There is zero difference between Alpha and Beta
Hint: Alpha is value added by active management; Beta is exposure to the market's moves.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Alpha (α) measures an active manager's value-add relative to a benchmark index, while Beta (β) measures the portfolio's systematic market risk.

Q3. In portfolio arithmetic, what is John Bogle's "Cost Matters Hypothesis"?
A. Gross market returns minus costs (fees, taxes, commissions) equal the net return shared by all investors, so low-cost indexing wins on average
B. Expensive funds always generate higher returns than cheap funds
C. Stockbrokers should receive 50% of investor profits
D. Printing paper statements costs more than digital servers
Hint: Vanguard founder John Bogle emphasized that in investing, you get what you don't pay for.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Jack Bogle showed that, as a group, all investors earn the gross market return; therefore low-cost indexers beat high-cost active investors on average, after fees.

Q4. What is "Survivorship Bias" in active mutual fund performance reporting?
A. Including only surviving, successful funds in historical averages while ignoring poorly performing funds that were closed or merged out of existence
B. Surviving an economic depression with physical gold
C. An insurance claim paid out to surviving family members
D. A tax audit applied exclusively to retired individuals
Hint: Failed, underperforming funds get shut down and vanish from databases, artificially inflating past average returns.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: Survivorship bias pushes historical fund averages upward because failed, underperforming funds are closed or merged, leaving mainly the better performers in the dataset.

Q5. What is an Exchange Traded Fund (ETF) and how does it differ mechanically from a standard open-ended Index Mutual Fund?
A. ETFs cannot hold stocks or bonds
B. ETFs trade continuously on stock exchanges throughout the day at live market prices, whereas index mutual funds are bought and sold once a day at the closing NAV
C. ETFs are managed exclusively by foreign governments
D. ETFs expire within 24 hours of purchase
Hint: ETFs trade like individual shares on the NSE and BSE during market hours.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: While both track indices passively, ETFs trade during the day on stock exchanges with real-time prices, whereas mutual fund units are bought or redeemed at the end-of-day NAV.
