Q1. What is the fundamental difference between a Call Option and a Put Option?
A. Call options are only for gold; Put options are for silver
B. A Call Option gives the buyer the right to buy the underlying asset at a set price, while a Put Option gives the right to sell it at a set price
C. Call options never expire; Put options expire in 10 minutes
D. There is no difference between them
Hint: Call = right to buy; Put = right to sell.
Correct Answer: B
Concept / Fact Explanation: A Call option gives the right to buy an asset at the strike price, while a Put option gives the right to sell it at the strike price.

## Explore Further
Q1. In options risk management, what does "Delta" (Δ) measure?
A. The time left until the contract expires
B. How much the option's premium is expected to change for a ₹1 change in the price of the underlying asset
C. The total trading volume on the exchange
D. The tax rate on company profits
Hint: Delta measures how much the option price moves per unit move in the underlying asset.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Delta (Δ) estimates the change in an option's premium for every ₹1 move in the underlying asset's price.

Q2. What is "Tracking Difference" in ETF analysis, compared with "Tracking Error"?
A. Tracking Difference is the yearly standard deviation of daily return differences; Tracking Error is the total return gap over a period
B. Tracking Difference is the total gap in return between the ETF and its index over a holding period, whereas Tracking Error measures how much that gap varies
C. Tracking Difference only applies to physical gold
D. There is no difference
Hint: Difference measures the size of the return gap; Error measures how much that gap jumps around.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Tracking Difference is the fund's total under- or over-performance against its index over time, while Tracking Error is the standard deviation (volatility) of the daily return differences.

Q3. What is the "Barbell Strategy" in asset allocation?
A. Exercising at the gym before trading stocks
B. Pairing two extremes — very safe, liquid assets on one end and high-growth assets on the other — while avoiding middle-risk assets
C. Investing 100% in steel plants
D. Borrowing 10x leverage on every trade
Hint: It loads up both ends of the risk spectrum (maximum safety + high upside) and skips the middle.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: Popularised by Nassim Nicholas Taleb, the Barbell Strategy combines very conservative, liquid assets with high-upside growth assets, avoiding middle-risk exposures.

Q4. In factor investing, what is the "Size factor" (small-cap premium), and what trade-off does it carry?
A. Smaller companies always go bankrupt within 12 months
B. Small-cap stocks have historically delivered higher long-term returns thanks to their room to grow, but with higher volatility and deeper falls in recessions
C. The size factor measures the floor area of company factories
D. Small-cap stocks never trade on stock exchanges
Hint: Higher long-term growth potential comes with bigger price swings.
Correct Answer: B
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Size factor captures the historical small-company premium — higher long-term returns in exchange for more volatility, lower liquidity, and bigger drawdowns.

Q5. What is the "Kelly Criterion" in capital allocation and risk management?
A. A formula for the best fraction of your capital to put into a series of favourable bets, to maximise long-term growth while avoiding ruin
B. A government tax code on foreign remittances
C. A central bank rule for printing banknotes
D. An insurance contract for home loans
Hint: It calculates the best bet size from the chance of winning and the payout odds.
Correct Answer: A
Fact / Concept Explanation: The Kelly Criterion, f* = (b × p − q) ÷ b (where b is the payout odds, p the chance of winning and q = 1 − p), gives the bet size that maximises the long-term growth rate of capital.
