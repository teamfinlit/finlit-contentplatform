# Needs vs Wants
**Level:** 1–2 Money Foundation | **Chapter:** 1 - What Is Money? | **Topic:** 4

## Key Concepts
- Needs
- Wants
- Essential vs Non-essential
- 24-Hour Rule
- Smart Decision Framework
- Spending Tracking

## Quick Spark
• Needs are essential items you must have to survive, while wants are nice-to-have extras.• The boundary between needs and wants can get blurry, requiring smart choices.• Pausing for 24 hours before buying non-essentials stops emotional impulse spending.• Tracking expenses gives you control over your financial freedom!

## Understand It
• Needs (Essential Survival Items)Needs represent the fundamental physical requirements necessary for maintaining basic health, shelter, and security. Basic food, clean water, adequate clothing, safe housing, essential education, and vital healthcare fall into this non-negotiable category. Without fulfilling these essentials, everyday well-being is directly at risk.Example: Rudy needs a balanced daily lunch, a warm school uniform, and basic medicines when unwell to stay healthy and active in class.• Wants (Nice-to-Have Extras)Wants encompass items, activities, and upgrades that make life more comfortable, entertaining, or enjoyable, but are not required for living. Video games, dining at fancy restaurants, branded designer apparel, and luxury gadgets are desirable wants. While enjoying wants is fun, fulfilling them should never compromise your ability to cover essential needs.Example: Maanu enjoys playing the latest video games and going to amusement parks on weekends, but not having them will not harm her safety or health.• Essential vs. Non-Essential (The Blurry Line)The boundary between a need and a want frequently blurs because modern tools can contain both essential utility and optional luxury upgrades. A dependable mobile phone or basic laptop is an essential need for schoolwork and family safety, but the most expensive premium flagship device is a want. Learning to separate basic function from luxury features is the core of financial discipline.Example: Dia needs sturdy running shoes for her daily walks, but buying a limited-edition designer sneaker that costs ten times more is fulfilling a want, not the basic need.• The 24-Hour RuleWhen you feel an intense urge to make an unplanned purchase for a non-essential item, delaying the purchase by exactly 24 hours lets emotional excitement settle. By waiting a full day, your logical brain evaluates whether the item provides genuine lasting value or was just a momentary impulse. This simple pause prevents buyers' remorse.Example: Rudy sees a shiny remote-controlled car in a store window and wants to buy it immediately; Dia suggests waiting 24 hours, and by next evening, Rudy realizes he would rather save for a bicycle.• Smart Decision Framework & Spending TrackingTracking every expenditure across weekly or monthly intervals lets you see exactly how much cash flows toward essentials versus impulse upgrades. Applying a disciplined budgeting balance (like making sure essentials and savings come first) ensures you never run short for critical bills while still enjoying guilt-free leisure. Knowing where money goes is the first step toward mastering it.Example: CA Jay helps Maanu list her monthly pocket-money expenses in a small notebook, revealing that daily canteen snacks were consuming cash she planned to save for music lessons.

## Real-Life Fact
In personal financial planning, the widely recognized 50-30-20 Rule suggests allocating approximately 50% of after-tax income to fundamental Needs, 30% to personal Wants, and 20% to Savings and debt clearance.

## Common Mistake
Common Mistake: Assuming that anything that makes daily life easier or feels exciting is automatically an essential need.Correction: Needs are limited to basic necessities for survival and health; luxury upgrades, extra features, and designer labels are optional wants.

## Key Takeaway
Prioritize your essentials first, enjoy your wants mindfully, and pause before spending impulsively.
